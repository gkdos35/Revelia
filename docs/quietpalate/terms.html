What QuietPalate does

It reads the ingredients you scan, type or choose from a restaurant menu, and shows what the research reviewed for this library says about each name. It keeps your own list of what bothers you and what is fine for you, on your device.

What QuietPalate is not

QuietPalate is an information tool for general wellbeing. It is not a medical device, and it is not intended to diagnose, treat, cure or prevent any disease. Using it does not make us your clinician and does not create any clinician-patient relationship.

It does not diagnose anything, interpret your symptoms or photos, or tell you to start, stop or change a medicine, a treatment or dental work. It does not replace your dentist, oral medicine specialist or doctor. Keep your check-ups, and ask them before making a big change. If something in your mouth is getting worse, that is a conversation for a clinician, not for an app. If you think you have a medical emergency, call your local emergency number or go to an emergency department.

What the library can and cannot tell you

The library reads for one thing: mouth symptoms in oral lichen planus, oral lichenoid lesions and irritation of sore tissue inside the mouth. It is not an allergy check. It does not look for the ingredients behind a food allergy in the body or for gluten in celiac disease, and an answer here says nothing about either. If you live with one of those as well, read the label for it exactly as you would without this app.

The library is a reviewed body of published research, not a complete one. A name shown as having no reports means no applicable report was connected to it in the research reviewed for this library. It is not a search done for that name, and it is never a finding that the name is safe. A name the app cannot recognize is shown as such rather than guessed at.

Recipes change, labels change and restaurant menus change, often without notice. The package in your hand and the restaurant you are standing in are always the more current source, so read the label before you buy or eat something, even when the app has already shown you an answer.

A camera scan can misread small or curved print, a barcode can bring back an older or different package than the one in your hand, and a label word that stands for a blend, such as flavor or spices, hides what is inside it.

The app also cannot know what a kitchen does out of sight, including cross-contact between foods. Use it to narrow things down, and your own experience and your clinician to decide.

What is saved?

Recent checks, saved labels, unfinished text reviews, restaurant pins, your list and which terms you agreed to are encrypted locally and configured to be excluded from ordinary backup and device-transfer paths. There is no account, cloud sync, advertising, or owner-operated health-data analytics.

In more detail, the app keeps these on your phone:

  • Recent checks and saved labels: the ingredient words you reviewed, the product name, brand, barcode and market if you have them, where a listing came from, and what the app read in it.
  • Unfinished text reviews, so a check you started can be picked up again.
  • Restaurant pins: the restaurants you chose to keep on Home.
  • Your list: the ingredients you marked as bothering you or as fine for you, any note you wrote beside one, and dishes you chose to remember.
  • Which terms you agreed to: the version of the Terms of use you agreed to on the welcome screen, and when. It is kept so the app can ask again if the terms change. It is never sent anywhere.
  • A few settings, such as whether you have finished the welcome, and the last thing you removed, so it can be undone.

The record is encrypted with AES-256-GCM. Its key is held by your phone’s own key storage (the Keychain on iPhone, the Keystore on Android) and is bound to that phone. The app excludes the record from iCloud and Android backups and from device-to-device transfer on purpose, so a new phone starts empty. To keep a copy of your list, open My list and use Share; on a new phone, you can bring that copy back in (see What do I choose to send?). These controls reduce exposure; they cannot guarantee protection against every operating-system, device-management, or forensic path.

What happens to photos?

A camera photo is used for on-device text recognition. A photo chosen from your library stays in your library. The app saves the recognized ingredient text, not either image.

The app asks for the camera only when you choose to photograph a label or scan a barcode. It reads a library photo only when you pick one. Text is read on the phone by Google ML Kit and, on iPhone, also by Apple’s own on-device text recognition. Barcodes are read on the phone too. Camera photos, and any copy the app makes while reading a photo, are deleted when the app is done with them. If the phone does not allow that right away, the app tries again while it is open and, in most cases, at its next start. If the phone keeps refusing, or the app is closed just as a copy is made, the file can stay in the app’s own temporary storage, which other apps cannot read, until the phone clears it or the app is removed.

How do I delete my data?

Open Settings, then Delete local data. This removes recent checks, saved labels, drafts, pins, your list, which terms you agreed to and undo history from this device. It cannot be undone.

Deleting also removes the record’s encryption key, and the welcome screen returns. Uninstalling the app removes its data the way your phone’s operating system removes any app’s data. Neither deletes an email you already sent, a copy you shared, or anything an outside service received.

What goes online?

When you choose barcode lookup, the barcode and ordinary connection information (including your IP address) go to Open Food Facts and Open Beauty Facts. For some products the barcode also goes to the maker’s own ingredient pages (Colgate-Palmolive, Procter & Gamble), and when none of those has an ingredient list, to the US Food and Drug Administration’s drug-label service. These services do not receive your personal records or label photos. Opening a source website uses your browser. Opening the support page adds the app version and your operating-system version to the web address, so a reply can match the build you are on; nothing else about you is added. OCR and typed-text checks remain available without product lookup. Google ML Kit processes scanned images, text and results on device. Its SDK sends app and device information, a per-installation identifier, performance, configuration, event and error metrics for diagnostics and usage analytics. It does not receive your label image, recognized text or recognition result.

Barcode lookup. It happens only when you scan or type a barcode and ask for it. Each request carries the barcode, the app’s name and version, and the address of the QuietPalate support page, so the service can tell which app is asking. It carries nothing from your list and no photo. Requests go over an encrypted connection to:

  • Open Food Facts (world.openfoodfacts.org) and Open Beauty Facts (world.openbeautyfacts.org), two non-profit, community-built product databases, for every lookup. See the Open Food Facts privacy policy and the Open Beauty Facts privacy policy.
  • Colgate-Palmolive’s public SmartLabel pages (www.colgatepalmolive.com), only for Colgate and Tom’s of Maine products.
  • Procter & Gamble’s product-ingredient service (az-na-smartlabel-prod-functionapp-api.pgcloud.com), only for Crest, Oral-B and Scope products.
  • The US Food and Drug Administration’s openFDA drug-label service (api.fda.gov), only when none of the above returned an ingredient list. The request also carries the drug code worked out from the barcode’s own digits.

Each service handles the request under its own policies. The app keeps a product it found in memory for up to 30 minutes, so scanning the same pack again is quick; that copy is gone when the app closes. A label you choose to save keeps the listing’s words and source on your phone, with the rest of your record.

Google ML Kit. Google states that the image, text and recognition output stay on the device, and that its SDK sends the diagnostic information described above to Google over an encrypted connection, without sharing it with third parties. Read Google’s ML Kit terms and privacy information. Sabaset Studios does not receive this information.

Pages you open. Links to sources, to organizations that write about lichen planus, and to the QuietPalate website open in your own browser, under that site’s terms and privacy policy.

What do I choose to send?

Nothing below happens unless you tap the button for it. Share hands the words on screen to your phone’s share sheet. Bringing back a shared list, and Paste when you type a list, read the clipboard only when you tap Paste, and send nothing. Reports open a drafted email in your own email app; you see it before you send it.

In more detail, and the app never picks where anything goes:

  • Share. Share on My list, on a label or on a dish hands the words on screen to your phone’s share sheet. Your list’s copy includes your notes and the dates you added each entry. Where it goes from there is your choice.
  • Bringing back a shared list. On a new phone, or after deleting your data, you can paste the copy you shared and the app reads it back into your list. It reads the clipboard only when you tap Paste, and it sends nothing.
  • Pasting an ingredient list. Paste on the typed-list screen reads the clipboard only when you tap it, and the words stay on your phone.
  • Reports and restaurant requests. These open a drafted email in your own email app, addressed to support. The draft names what the report is about (for a restaurant, the restaurant, the item and what looks wrong), any note you typed, and the app version. You see it before you send it, and you can change or delete anything in it. If no email app opens, the app offers to copy the draft so you can paste it into an email yourself.

Support email

If you write to support, Sabaset Studios receives your email address, your message, and anything you attach. That is used to reply, to look into the report, and to keep a record of how it was resolved. Email is handled by your email provider and ours under their own policies.

Please do not send mouth or lesion photos, medical or dental records, or a full health history. The QuietPalate support page has a short report template.

What Sabaset Studios does not collect

Through the app, Sabaset Studios:

  • does not require an account
  • does not run a server that receives your checks, saved labels, or list
  • does not provide cloud sync
  • does not include advertising SDKs or any analytics of its own
  • does not track people across apps or websites
  • does not sell or share personal information
  • does not receive label photos, recognized text, or your list, except what you choose to send yourself

Apple and Google may report crashes and app performance to developers when you have allowed diagnostics sharing in your phone’s settings. That is their service, under their privacy policies, and it does not include your records.

Children

QuietPalate is made for adults; the Terms of use set the age at 18, with a parent or guardian agreeing for anyone younger. Sabaset Studios collects nothing from anyone through the app, so it collects nothing from children. What Google ML Kit sends to Google is described under What goes online?

The QuietPalate website

The website’s pages have no analytics, advertising, tracking scripts, contact forms, or embedded third-party widgets. They are hosted with GitHub Pages. GitHub states that it logs a visitor’s IP address for security purposes when a GitHub Pages site is visited. See GitHub Pages data collection. When the app opens the support page, GitHub Pages receives the requested address, including the app version and operating-system version described above.
